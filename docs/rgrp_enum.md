## 6. R-Group Enumeration

**Example:** `examples/rgrp_enum_run.ipynb`

Given the SMILES of the entire molecule along with the SMILES of the scaffold present within it, the code identifies the attached R-groups and replaces them with similar fragments from the fragment library.

The selection and replacement of fragments are carried out through the following steps:

1. **Initial shortlisting:** Similar fragments are selected from the fragment library loaded from Hugging Face. Two types of similarity-based selection are available:

   - **Descriptor-based (`desc`):** Since the replacement fragments are generally smaller in size, they need to retain the necessary physicochemical properties while also fitting within the available pocket space. Hence, min-max filters are applied based on the properties of the reference R-group being replaced.The current property ranges are:
        - Heavy atoms: -3 to +2
        - Rings: 0
        - HBD: 0
        - HBA: -1 to +1
        - TPSA: -15 to +15
        - Fsp3: -0.25 to +0.25
        - Number of branches (`n_branch`): -1 to +2

   - **ECFP4-based (`ecfp4`):** Uses ECFP4-based Tanimoto similarity between the reference R-group being replaced and the fragments in the library.

2. **Pharmacophore-based ranking:** The shortlisted fragments are further ranked based on the similarity of their pharmacophore arrangement around the attachment point with respect to the reference R-group.

3. **Fragment selection:** Based on the ranking, the top fragments are selected for each R-group site using the specified `cutoff` value.

4. **Combination generation:** All possible combinations of the shortlisted fragments across the different R-group sites are generated.

5. **Molecule generation:** The selected fragments are attached at their corresponding positions in the original molecule to generate the final set of molecules.

**Note:** The final molecules are not ranked based on any particular scoring parameter. They are simply the output of the enumeration pipeline, with no additional ranking applied after molecule generation.

### Inputs

- `whole_smiles`: SMILES of the entire molecule.
- `scaffold_smiles`: SMILES of the scaffold used to identify the R-group attachment sites.
- `cutoff`: Number of fragments to shortlist for each R-group being replaced.
- `fragment_lib`: Fragment library containing the fragments and their physicochemical properties.
- `sim_mode`: Determines the similarity-based selection method. Options: `"desc"` / `"ecfp4"`.
- `output_dir`: Directory where the outputs are stored.

### Fragment Library

The fragment library is generated by performing BRICS fragmentation on approximately 2 million ChEMBL molecules available through TDC.

The resulting fragments are screened to remove:
- Molecules containing PAINS substructures.
- Molecules containing Brenk structural alerts.
- Inorganic molecules.
- Molecules containing metals.

The library is then used for similarity-based fragment selection and subsequent R-group enumeration.
